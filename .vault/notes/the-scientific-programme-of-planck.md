## Summary
This paper describes the scientific objectives of the Planck mission, the third space-based cosmic microwave background mission following COBE and WMAP. Planck was designed to extract essentially all information from CMB temperature anisotropies and measure polarization with unprecedented accuracy, enabling probes of the early universe and potential detection of gravitational wave signatures from inflation.

## Key Facts
- Planck is the third space CMB mission after COBE and WMAP
- The mission measures both temperature anisotropies and polarization of the cosmic microwave background
- Polarization measurements encode information about the thermal history when the first stars and galaxies formed
- CMB polarization may detect the stochastic gravitational wave background from inflation occurring 10^-35 seconds after the Big Bang
- The satellite surveys the whole sky with unprecedented frequency coverage, angular resolution, and sensitivity

Authors:The [Planck Collaboration](https://arxiv.org/search/astro-ph?searchtype=author&query=Planck+Collaboration)

> Abstract: For 40 years, the cosmic microwave background (CMB) has been the most important source of information about the geometry and contents of the Universe. Even so, only a small fraction of the information available in the CMB has been extracted to date. Planck, the third space CMB mission after COBE and WMAP, is designed to extract essentially all of the information in the CMB temperature anisotropies. Planck will also measure to high accuracy the polarization of CMB anisotropies, which encodes not only a wealth of cosmological information but also provides a unique probe of the thermal history of the Universe during the time when the first stars and galaxies formed. Polarization measurements may also detect the signature of a stochastic background of gravitational waves generated during inflation, 10^(-35) s after the Big Bang. This book describes the expected scientific output of the Planck mission, both cosmological and non-cosmological. Chapter 1 summarizes the experimental concept and the operation of the satellite. Chapter 2 covers the core cosmological science of the mission, describing the measurements that Planck will make, what we expect to learn from them about the geometry and contents of the Universe and about fundamental physics, and the combination of CMB data with other data to provide additional insights. Although the primary goal of Planck is cosmology, it will survey the whole sky with an unprecedented combination of frequency coverage, angular resolution, and sensitivity, providing data valuable for a broad range of astrophysics. Chapters 3, 4, and 5 describe non-cosmological astrophysical uses of the Planck data. This book can also be downloaded directly from [this http URL](http://www.rssd.esa.int/Planck) .
